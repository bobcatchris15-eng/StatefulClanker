## 0. How to read this ledger

### 0.1 The eight fields of every row

Every row below carries all eight fields required by `CLANKER_COLLECTIVE_INTELLIGENCE_AUDIT.md` §20 and by my task's acceptance criteria, in this order:

1. **Claim id** — stable, prefixed by the section of the cross-cohort synthesis it comes from (`C-1xx` strengths, `C-2xx` systemic friction, `C-3xx` repeated rediscovery, `C-4xx` information loss, `C-5xx` cooperation, `C-6xx` dispute/minority, `C-7xx` primitive, `C-8xx` rejection). The prefix is a locator, not a ranking.
2. **Description** — the claim, stated so a reader can decide whether they agree with it.
3. **Supporting raw reports** — the *independent worker* reports that observed it, by identifier, as recorded in the syntheses. Control-plane material is **never** in this field (see 0.2).
4. **Contradicting reports** — or, where no report contradicts it, the explicit words **"none — no report in A–G contradicts this claim"**, so that *absence of contradiction* is distinguishable from *unchecked contradiction*.
5. **Code / runtime evidence** — paths, line numbers, symbols, task or receipt identifiers, or observed runtime behaviour. Rows citing no source at all say so.
6. **Confidence** — on the vocabulary declared in `cross-cohort-findings.md` §0.4: `high` / `medium-high` / `medium` / `low`, and `low` is additionally flagged **minority** where a single report carries it uncorroborated.
7. **Unresolved caveats** — the thing most likely to be wrong, the scope limit, or the measurement nobody has made.
8. **Recommended next experiment or change** — the specific falsifying experiment, deterministic check, or change, and, per my task, **the specific contradicting evidence, check or result that would retire the claim** (recorded for every row in §10, and cross-referenced from each row).

### 0.2 The counting rule, and the one discount that governs every number here

A support number counts **distinct independent worker raw reports** across Cohorts A–G, cited by identifier. **Control-plane material is never counted** (`EXP-AUDIT-CONTROL-PLANE-PARTICIPATES`).

**Three things weaken every count in this ledger, and none of them is a measurement.** I state them here once, in the ledger's own front matter, because they travel with every row that follows:

1. **Coverage, not ensemble.** A cohort of eight workers is eight *slices*, not eight estimates of a quantity. "7 of 8 agreed" means "seven slices reported the same thing from different files." `cross-cohort-findings.md` §0.1(1) forbids the phrase "N of 8 agreed" without that distinction; I follow it. Where a support number appears below I have written it as *"N reports, N cohorts"* rather than as a fraction that reads like a vote.
2. **The corpus is a tree, not eight independent observations.** Cohorts A–D ran under instruction-level enforcement; E, F and G ran from a sanitized base. Cohort C's synthesis records 2 of 8 workers contaminated despite instruction-only enforcement. The contamination rates are **not comparable across cohorts and must not be pooled** (`cross-cohort-findings.md` §0.1(2)).
3. **The questions were chosen by the authority that authored the prior syntheses.** `adversarial-synthesis.md` §12.1 quotes the orchestrator's own in-advance admission that it selected the E and F slice questions from its own interim A–D synthesis; G-05 reached the same conclusion by inference. **Consequence: agreement between the control plane and the workers is discounted; divergence is weighted up** (`cross-cohort-findings.md` §0.1(3), §6-2). I apply that asymmetry throughout and nowhere do I count a control-plane confirmation as worker corroboration.

### 0.3 Disposition is not confidence

The adversarial phase's disposition ledger is authoritative for what survived (`cross-cohort-findings.md` §0.5, §8A). A claim can be **high confidence and killed** — the kill is about cost, scope or placement, not about truth. G-07's sentence is the standard I apply to every row: *"Every attack below is an attack on cost and placement, not on fact."* Rows therefore carry a disposition tag independently of confidence:

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `[SURVIVED]` | attacked and not broken; carried forward as a recommendation candidate |
| `[DAMAGED]` | the observation survives, the recommendation as specified does not — narrowed or re-costed |
| `[KILLED]` | the recommendation no longer exists; the row is retained *because the kill and its evidence are themselves a claim* |
| `[SIMPLIFIED]` | a smaller mechanism, or an existing carrier, delivers most of the benefit |
| `[UNRESOLVED]` | the adversarial phase explicitly declined to close it; a later vote must not |
| `[NEGATIVE]` | a finding that the current design is **adequate** — first-class per brief §6/§17, and the class most likely to be lost in tidying |

### 0.4 What I am not doing

I did not re-derive the cross-cohort synthesis; I am not adjudicating between the cohorts' syntheses; I am not proposing a mechanism (that is `COORDINATION_PROTOCOL.md`'s slice); and I am not applying anything. The ledger's job is narrower: **every important claim gets a row, every row is traceable to the reports that carried it, and every row says what would retire it.** A reconciliation that quietly re-admits a killed recommendation has failed this artifact, so §8 reproduces the kill register in full.
