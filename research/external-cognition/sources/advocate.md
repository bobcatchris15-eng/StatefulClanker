# Advocate source ledger

Research access date: 2026-10-01. “Verified” means the cited primary/official source page or full paper was inspected for the stated claim. URLs checked via web search/open in this work session. Scholarly primary sources are weighted above forum practice. Temporal status is historical for published studies; forum is current-to-date practitioner discussion.

1. **Nye et al., “Show Your Work: Scratchpads for Intermediate Computation with Language Models” (2021), Google Research publication page.** https://research.google/pubs/show-your-work-scratchpads-for-intermediate-computation-with-language-models/ — **Type:** primary empirical research, publication-host page. **Verified claim:** lines 128–130 say models struggle with unbounded multi-step computation, but few-shot models can do complex multi-step computation when asked to show intermediate results; trained Transformers emit scratchpad steps, improving tasks from long addition to arbitrary program execution. **Limit:** adds sequential generated tokens; work concerns computation and does not isolate an external persistent store or demonstrate performance beyond an independently measured capacity ceiling. **Temporal:** historical result.

2. **Chen et al., “Program of Thoughts Prompting: Disentangling Computation from Reasoning for Numerical Reasoning Tasks” (TMLR 2023; arXiv preprint 2022).** https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12588 — **Type:** primary empirical research / full paper. **Verified claim:** abstract and sections 1–2 state the LM expresses reasoning as a program, while external Python performs computation; evaluates five math word-problem and three financial QA datasets; reports about 12% average performance gain over CoT in the abstract (body breaks out setting-specific gains). **Limit:** numerical-domain benchmark; execution capability and extra compute are integral; no measured representational threshold. **Temporal:** historical result.

3. **Yao et al., “Tree of Thoughts: Deliberate Problem Solving with Large Language Models” (NeurIPS 2023).** https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2023/hash/271db9922b8d1f4dd7aaef84ed5ac703-Abstract.html — **Type:** primary conference paper / official proceedings. **Verified claim:** abstract says ToT explores coherent intermediate “thoughts,” considers multiple reasoning paths, self-evaluates, looks ahead and backtracks; results on Game of 24, creative writing, and Mini Crosswords. The full paper reports Game of 24 4% with GPT-4 CoT versus 74% ToT. **Limit:** more search/model calls/tokens; state is inference-time branches rather than durable cross-reset representation. Three tasks are narrow. **Temporal:** historical result.

4. **Juneja et al., “Small Language Models Fine-tuned to Coordinate Larger Language Models improve Complex Reasoning” (EMNLP 2023).** https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.225/ — **Type:** primary ACL conference paper. **Verified claim:** abstract says problem decomposition and solution generation are distinct capabilities better addressed by separate modules; DaSLaM has a decomposition generator split complex problems into subproblems answered by a solver. **Limit:** task routing/decomposition, not proof of externalized mutable state; fine-tuned small coordinator, not necessarily same model after context reset. **Temporal:** historical result.

5. **Wang et al., “Towards Understanding Chain-of-Thought Prompting: An Empirical Study of What Matters” (ACL 2023).** https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.153/ — **Type:** primary ACL conference paper. **Verified claim:** abstract says invalid reasoning demonstrations can retain over 80–90% of correct-CoT performance by various metrics; relevance to the question and correct ordering are more important. **Limit:** the abstract doesn't report raw task breakdown in these lines; this cautions against treating chain text as faithful reasoning, while showing order/relevance effects. CoT prompting isn't persistent external state. **Temporal:** historical result.

6. **Kirsh & Maglio, “On Distinguishing Epistemic from Pragmatic Action” (Cognitive Science 18, 1994).** https://doi.org/10.1207/S15516709COG1804_1 — **Type:** primary cognitive-science study and theoretical article. **Verified claim:** publisher abstract says data/argument from Tetris show some cognitive/perceptual problems are solved more quickly, easily, and reliably by actions in the world than by computations in the head alone; abstract defines epistemic actions as revealing information hidden or hard to compute mentally. **Limit:** human interactive Tetris behavior; not an AI model and not a generic external store. **Temporal:** historical result.

7. **Laland et al., “Intelligent problem-solvers externalize cognitive operations” (Nature Human Behaviour, 2019; PubMed abstract).** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30944451/ — **Type:** primary human behavioral study. **Verified/reported claim:** abstract describes active task-environment manipulation in Raven Advanced Progressive Matrices; environment-altering actions clustered between periods of apparent inactivity; first sample's interactive click-and-drag test better predicted academic achievement than conventional static test, partially replicated in a novel sample. **Limit:** predicts individual differences and describes alternating internal/external behavior; does not show manipulation itself universally improves performance or support model transfer. **Temporal:** historical result.

8. **Hugging Face forum discussion “How do you design memory systems for long-running AI agents?” (post begun 2026-04-27).** https://discuss.huggingface.co/t/how-do-you-design-memory-systems-for-long-running-ai-agents/175584 — **Type:** technical forum discussion (practitioner report/opinion; non-peer-reviewed). **Verified claim:** thread posts recommend runtime-owned memory, small per-call active context, and treating prompt as a working view of external state; replies discuss stored prompt/response files and audit/erasure concerns. **Limit:** no experimental design or performance measurements; participant report is not independent verification. **Temporal:** current-to-date forum discourse, revalidate for current practice.

## Evidence taxonomy

- “Verified” above refers to whether an authoritative page/full paper supports the literal reported description, not whether the proposed interpretation is proven.
- The analogy from human active externalization to model-assisted external state is **inferred**, not verified as a cross-species/cross-architecture causal fact.
- That external state can enable model reasoning beyond a **measured model-specific capacity limit** remains **unknown** in these sources.
- Reported score gains are study-reported and benchmark-bounded; no independent replication was performed for this source note.
