# Methods validator — first-pass audit

Date: 2026-10-01. Scope: independent review of the frozen pilot protocol and primary-source claims, against the exact hypothesis in `HUMAN_HANDOFF.md`. I did not edit the protocol, fixtures, scorer, or production files.

## Verdict

The literature is represented with appropriately narrow caveats overall. The pilot can test whether handing a fresh solver a derived, typed constraint ledger helps it apply one new exclusion versus asking it to recompute from the original facts. It cannot test the distinctive clause in the human hypothesis: improvement for models unable to hold the full representation internally. It also does not isolate persistent external representation from transferred computation, because the treatment gets Phase 1's enumerated candidates and winner while the control gets all raw facts and must redo the search.

**Do not launch subjects until the frozen evaluator exists and is checked.** `protocol.md` requires `score.py` and `private/answer_key.json`, but neither is currently present. Scoring after outputs without a frozen evaluator/key risks post-hoc scoring and makes the preregistration incomplete.

## Consequential source checks

1. **Universality paper — verified, with a strict scope boundary.** Schuurmans' primary arXiv paper says the construction uses Flan-U-PaLM 540B, external associative read/write memory, and a stored-instruction/prompt program to simulate U15,2. The proof checks a finite set of model prompt behaviors (29 state/symbol cases), with greedy decoding. This supports formal computational expressivity for that engineered setup; it does not show natural-language state updates, reliable practical reasoning, smaller-model performance, or native-capacity extension. The skeptic ledger is accurate on this distinction. Source: [arXiv:2301.04589](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04589).

2. **MemoryAgentBench — verified, date needs precise labeling.** The authors' primary preprint (submitted July 7, 2025) defines accurate retrieval, test-time learning, long-range understanding, and selective forgetting, and reports evaluated methods do not master all four. The later OpenReview version is labeled ICLR 2026. Say “2025 preprint; published at ICLR 2026,” not simply a 2025 publication. This benchmark motivates multi-competency evaluation but does not establish a universal failure or test this pilot's hypothesis. Sources: [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.05257), [OpenReview paper](https://openreview.net/pdf/dca8178b2d4fb7cd70a435ed43b655c97ce9871c.pdf). The OpenReview URL should be replaced with a verified paper page/PDF URL before relying on the publication claim; the browser surfaced the PDF but its stable forum ID was not resolved in this pass.

3. **Lost in the Middle — verified and properly qualified.** The primary paper reports position-sensitive degradation on its multi-document QA and key-value retrieval tasks, including middle-position performance drops for tested long-context models. It is a historical, task-bounded result and cannot be treated as evidence of the target model's native capacity limit or as evidence that this state handoff fixes it. Source: [arXiv:2307.03172](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172).

4. **Hugging Face forum chronology — source note has a date error.** The primary thread shows the topic opened April 27, 2026; May 3 is the date of a reply, not the thread start. The practitioner recommendations are opinions/engineering advice and contain no controlled outcome data. Correct `sources/advocate.md`'s “post begun 2026-05-03” to April 27, 2026. Source: [Hugging Face discussion](https://discuss.huggingface.co/t/how-do-you-design-memory-systems-for-long-running-ai-agents/175584).

5. **Reddit benchmark critique — accurately anecdotal, but the inference should stay conditional.** The post dated August 26, 2026 describes some scripted conversations whose agent responses are recorded to an answer key. That raises a ground-truth design question; by itself it does not prove contamination or invalidity. A frozen key may instead assess self-consistency, while per-system keys impair cross-system comparability. Preserve this as a practitioner concern, not evidence that benchmark keys are wrong. Source: [r/AI_Agents post](https://www.reddit.com/r/AI_Agents/comments/1vyp3i5/please_critique_my_agent_memory_benchmark_exam/).

## Protocol issues that change what the experiment can say

- **One delta is vacuous in fixture B.** B makes E mandatory, H depends on C, and C conflicts with E. Therefore H is infeasible before the delta; making H unavailable removes no feasible candidate and cannot test state revision. Replace the delta with an exclusion that removes at least one previously feasible selection. Preferably choose a target that invalidates the pre-delta optimum in each fixture, and record the invariant expected to change.
- **Neither delta appears to change the optimum.** In A, G is not needed by the apparent optimum A/E/F/H (cost 8, value 21); in B, H was already infeasible. A still removes feasible selections involving G, but the winner is unchanged. Since winner-before/after is a scored component, include a case where the optimum must change if testing update quality.
- **Derived work is the treatment.** The state arm transfers a Phase 1 candidate ledger (potentially every feasible subset) and the winner. The control gets source facts and must repeat the enumeration. This is a useful comparison of “carry forward computed state” against “recompute from facts,” but it confounds external persistence with answer-bearing derived information and saved search work. The protocol acknowledges this limitation; report the estimand exactly that way. A follow-up should include an equal-call/token or compute-matched control and a fact-only external state.
- **No capacity condition is manipulated or measured.** This is a sensible choice because artificial clipping would not prove native incapacity, but it means no result from this pilot bears on whether a model cannot hold the full representation. A separate capacity curve on held-out generated instances is needed; only then test whether external-state benefit concentrates above a preregistered reliable-capacity threshold.
- **Scoring artifact absent and exact-ledger scoring is brittle.** `score.py` and the answer key named by the protocol are missing. Freeze them before subjects run, and ensure the ledger comparison rejects duplicate rows rather than converting both lists to sets. The exact “every feasible selection” output can be long; set a common output-token cap and record it, plus actual input/output token use. Invalid/truncated JSON is otherwise hard to distinguish from an output budget artifact.
- **Settings and execution fidelity remain underspecified.** Protocol says same route/settings/output limit/sampling throughout but does not assign values; it also says counterbalance “if possible.” Record actual model identifier, decoding configuration, max output tokens, tool access and call sequence before launch, and counterbalance deterministically. Keep the already stated weak-blinding/workspace-access caveat.

## Recommended interpretation

If executed after fixing the evaluator and the ineffective B delta, report only a two-fixture descriptive pilot about transferring derived constraint state across fresh calls. A higher treatment score would be evidence of utility for this task under its resource arrangement, not evidence that external state overcomes a model's native representational limit. At n=2, avoid significance or generality claims.


## Post-run audit addendum — 2026-10-01

### Completed pilot v1.1

I independently enumerated all 256 subsets for each eight-project fixture, enforcing mandatory inclusion, budget, dependency closure, incompatibilities, and availability. The generated before/after candidate ledgers and winners exactly match every `private/answer_key.json` entry: A has 19 feasible rows before and 14 after, with winners AEFGH (10, 27) and CEFH (9, 23); B has 10 before and 6 after, with winners DEFG (9, 26) and ABEG (7, 22). The frozen scorer's self-check passed, and its run gives the preregistered scores: state 90/90 (mean 90), control 90/80 (mean 85). All four Phase 2 outputs have exact ledger and winner under the independent oracle. The five-point composite difference is entirely a score-format issue in the exact patch/fact components; it is not an observed winner or ledger advantage.

The exact patch/fact score is not semantically stable under the written schema. The protocol asks for changed facts as a `{project_id, availability}` object but never defines the representation of availability inside `current_state.source_facts`; scorer requires an `unavailable` array. B-state and B-control use `availability: {"D":"unavailable"}`, which faithfully expresses the new fact but loses the 10-point fact component. Likewise, `removed_candidates` is described as a list of old selections, without specifying whether each element is an ID array or a full candidate row. A-state, A-control and B-control provide the full old rows (which identify exactly the right removed selections); the scorer only accepts ID arrays, causing the 10-point patch component to fail. B-state used ID arrays and passed. Preserve the preregistered scores, but do not interpret the 90-versus-85 gap as evidence of better state-condition reasoning. A semantic review finds both changed-fact representations correct and all four removed sets correct.

A-control Phase 2 reread the prompt and corrected its initial ledger before the final answer; the initial draft was not retained. This deviation is recorded in `execution_log.md`. It can affect compute and means the response history is incomplete; exact token/sampling data are not exposed, so this remains a limitation. Responses were under the procedural 20,000-character ceiling. Their sizes are 1,680–2,783 bytes; per-file sizes and SHA-256 digests are in the final review. No true sampler token ceiling or exact token count was available. Do not equate response bytes with model token cost.

### Bounded-view CSP chain

I ran `bounded_views/score.py` independently on the three saved responses. Stage 1 is accepted and exactly matches its frontier. Stage 2 is rejected: for E=1 it reports C=1,D=0,E=1, violating the frozen odd-parity condition (sum 2), and claims cost 1 even though E=1 costs 3. The exact E=1 frontier row is cost 3 with witness A=0,B=0,C=0,D=0,E=1. Stage 3 is rejected because it receives a rejected Stage 2 frontier; its proposed assignment also violates the stage-2 parity constraint and its reported total cost 2 is wrong. Independent whole-instance enumeration gives the exact optimum cost 4, assignment A=0,B=0,C=0,D=0,E=1,F=0,G=1. Thus the chain demonstrates one correct frontier followed by a failed transition and propagated invalid state; it does not demonstrate end-to-end advancement.

The checker computes exact expected values and returns a reject/accept judgment, but it is stateless: “preserve prior accepted state unchanged; commit no patch” is a printed policy message, not a durable transactional write. In this run the coordinator manually supplied each raw prior response, and Stage 3 was correctly rejected after Stage 2 failed. The test therefore demonstrates prompt-mediated handoff plus external validation, not an implemented transactional memory store or automatic rollback. The restriction to local slices is imposed by protocol and cannot support a claim of native inability to hold the full problem.

### Source audit carried forward

Primary source claims remain bounded and support the report's limited interpretation: Schuurmans' construction uses Flan-U-PaLM 540B with an associative read/write memory and an engineered stored-instruction/prompt loop, with finitely many prompt behaviors verified; MemoryAgentBench reports four distinct competencies and evaluated methods' shortcomings; Lost in the Middle is model/task-specific position degradation. The Hugging Face forum thread opened April 27, 2026; May 3 is a reply date. Forum material is practitioner advice, not outcome evidence. Direct citations are in the first-pass report.

## Adaptive recovery addendum — 2026-10-01

I reran the unchanged `bounded_views/score.py` against the two recovery outputs using the frozen Stage 1 response. Both are rejected. Recovery Stage 2 remains incorrect: its E=0 row is not the minimum-cost lexicographic frontier row (it gives A1/B0/C1/D0/E0 at cost 3; the correct row is A0/B0/C0/D1/E0 at cost 1). Its E=1 row uses A1/B0/C1/D0/E1, which violates the required odd-parity constraint, and reports cost 1 instead of the correct cost 3. Recovery Stage 3 repeats the earlier invalid assignment and cost 2; because Stage 2 was rejected, the chain is rejected as well. The exact global optimum remains cost 4, A0/B0/C0/D0/E1/F0/G1.

This is one adaptive, feedback-assisted recovery attempt with two fresh subjects, not a confirmatory repeat and not a rate estimate. It did not repair the transition. The total fresh subjects across the two original pilots and this follow-up is 13. The existing synthesis should replace “adaptive recovery pending” with this outcome and update the total; its substantive conclusions about the bounded chain, lack of native-capacity evidence, and no solution-accuracy advantage remain appropriately limited.
